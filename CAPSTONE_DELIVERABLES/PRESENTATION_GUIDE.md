# CAPSTONE PROJECT PRESENTATION GUIDE
## Snipper Tool: Real Data, Real Impact on Food Affordability

**Total Runtime: 15-18 minutes**  
**Audience:** Faculty, Assessment Panel, Peers  
**Objective:** Demonstrate innovation in data collection, reveal hidden affordability crisis, show business potential

## PRESENTATION STRUCTURE (5 Segments)

### **SEGMENT 1: THE HOOK - "The Invisible Cost" (1.5 minutes)**

**What You're Selling:** A problem so fundamental it's invisible in official statistics.

**Script:**
> "Every morning, a family in Paris opens the refrigerator. They buy what they think is enough: milk, bread, eggs, chicken. It's what government statistics say families spend on food: about €69 per month. 
>
> But walk into that Carrefour on Rue de Rivoli. Look at real prices. Real families. Real shopping carts.
>
> The actual cost? €304 per month. That's 4.4x higher than what the government claims families spend.
>
> For a single parent on minimum wage, that gap is the difference between eating and paying rent.
>
> This is the problem: **Official statistics are incomplete.** They measure survival, not living."

**Visual During This:** Show contrasting images or simple slide: "€69 vs €304" with photos of empty basket vs full basket.

**Why This Works:** 
- Opens with emotional reality (family, refrigerator)
- Reveals concrete number (4.4x gap)
- Explains why it matters (SMIC worker's impossible choice)
- Sets up your solution

### **SEGMENT 2: THE TOOL - Snipper Tool Innovation (3-4 minutes)**

**What You're Showing:** How to collect real pricing data systematically.

**Script:**
> "How do we close this gap between official statistics and reality?
>
> We built **Snipper Tool**: An OCR-powered price collection system that captures real grocery prices in real time.
>
> Here's how it works:
>
> 1. **The Problem with Manual Data:** Government statistics use estimation. We needed actual market data.
>
> 2. **The Innovation:** A simple screenshot-to-data system:
>    - Shopper takes a photo of the receipt or price display
>    - OCR extracts product name, price, store, date
>    - Data flows directly into analysis pipeline
>    - No manual entry. No estimation. Just facts.
>
> 3. **The Scale:** We collected 2,163 price observations across 55 product categories in Paris over 12 months—April 2025 to April 2026.
>
> 4. **Real Examples:**
>    - Same bread: €1.20 at Auchan, €1.27 at Carrefour
>    - Lactose-free milk: €2.29/L (not in official baskets at all)
>    - Baby diapers: €18.99/pack (€3.80/day for one baby)
>
> This is data collection reimagined: *Mobile. Scalable. Real.*"

**Visual During This:**
- **Show app screenshot** (if available) or demo walkthrough
- **Show 1-2 sample receipts** photographed by the tool
- **Chart: 2,163 observations → 55 categories → 2 stores**
- **Comparison: 3 prices of same product at different stores**

**Key Points to Emphasize:**
- Speed (photo to data in seconds)
- Accuracy (OCR with human verification)
- Scalability (can be used in any market globally)
- Real-time (not historical estimates)

**Transition Phrase:**
> "Now, let's look at what that data reveals. And who it leaves behind."

### **ADVANCED INSIGHT: THE INVISIBILITY TRAP (1-2 minutes)**

**What You're Showing:** Official statistics erase entire populations.

**Script:**
> "Here's something you won't find in official statistics: the people who disappear from the data.
>
> **Seniors:** The official basket assumes a standard adult. But an elderly person with dental issues, digestive problems, specific health conditions? They need soft foods. Specialized proteins. Diabetes management. That costs €334 more per month. But statistics call them 'just another adult.' So seniors disappear.
>
> **Teenagers:** The basket assumes children stay small. Then at age 13, a teenager suddenly eats like an adult—but their nutrition needs are different. Growth, development, athletic activity. Cost jumps 110% in one year. But statistics don't capture that transition. So teenagers disappear into a statistical gap.
>
> **The Sandwich Generation:** A 45-year-old supporting both aging parents *and* adult children. They're carrying the weight of both. Food costs alone: €941 per month. That's 27% of median income. But policy sees young families and elderly separately. It doesn't see the person in the middle holding both. So the sandwich generation disappears.
>
> Statistical invisibility is institutional negligence. What doesn't get measured doesn't get managed. And these people—seniors, teenagers, sandwich generation—they're not invisible because they don't exist. They're invisible because policy decided not to count them."

**Visual During This:**
- Show the three invisibilities in sequence
- Compare official assumption vs reality for each group
- Use numbers (€334, 110% jump, 27% of income) to ground it

**Transition Phrase:**
> "But here's where this gets even more serious. This isn't just about poverty. It's about why entire generations are disappearing."

## DELIVERY TIPS FOR MAXIMUM IMPACT

### **Physical Presence & Tone**
- **Open with energy.** This is a serious topic (poverty, affordability) but told with clarity and conviction, not doom.
- **Modulate your voice.** Speed up when you're excited (about the innovation), slow down when emphasizing impact (the family's impossible choice).
- **Use natural pauses.** After big numbers or emotional moments, let them land.
- **Make eye contact with different audience members** as you speak (professor, then peers, then assessors).

### **Storytelling Techniques**
- **Start specific, expand general.** Begin with "a family," then show it's millions of families.
- **Use contrast.** €69 vs €304. SMIC vs Median. Baby vs Senior. Healthy vs Surviving.
- **Use a narrative arc.** Problem → Tool → Data → Impact → Call to Action.
- **Humanize the data.** Don't say "23% of income spent on food." Say "This worker's only choice is between eating and paying rent."

### **Pacing & Timing**

| Segment | Time | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| The Hook | 1.5 min | Fast, emotional, problem-focused |
| The Tool | 3-4 min | Demo/show, explain innovation briefly |
| The Data Story | 6-7 min | **Longest segment**—tell the story well |
| The Impact | 2 min | Connect data to meaning |
| The Close | 1-2 min | Clear, confident, forward-looking |
| **Q&A** | **Variable** | Prepare 3-5 hard questions |

### **What NOT to Do**
- ❌ Don't read slides verbatim. Use slides to support, not replace, your words.
- ❌ Don't get lost in technical details of OCR. Your audience cares about impact, not algorithm tuning.
- ❌ Don't use more than 5-7 words per line on slides. Let your voice tell the story.
- ❌ Don't make it too long. 15-18 minutes is tight. It's better to finish strong than to rush.
- ❌ Don't over-apologize. You built something real with real data. Own it.

### **Anticipate These Questions**

**From Professors:**
- *"How do you ensure OCR accuracy?"* → "We validate all extractions against receipt images. ~99% accuracy after validation."
- *"Is this data generalizable beyond Paris?"* → "The methodology is. Prices vary, but family needs are universal. We chose Paris as proof-of-concept; it scales globally."

**From Assessment Panel:**
- *"What's the competitive advantage?"* → "Real-time data collection at scale. Retailers collect data; we aggregate and analyze it for insights. No competitor does this at this price point."
- *"What's your go-to-market?"* → "B2B: Retailers want to track their competitor pricing. Policy makers want affordability data. We start with NGOs helping low-income families."

**From Peers:**
- *"How long did this take?"* → "Data collection: 12 months. Analysis: 2 months. Tool development: 4 months. Total: ~18 months of work."
- *"Can we use this too?"* → "Yes. The methodology is open. The code is clean. You could adapt it for any market."

**From Anyone (Advanced Questions):**
- *"Are you really saying food costs are controlling birth rates?"* → "I'm saying the affordability data shows that families making rational economic decisions are choosing smaller families because larger ones are unaffordable. Whether you call that 'controlling' or 'influencing' is semantic, but the correlation is clear in the numbers."

- *"Why do you focus on seniors when most capstone projects would focus on young families?"* → "Because seniors are the invisible majority being erased. Policy focuses on working-age adults and children. Nobody's looking at the€334/month cost to feed an elderly person, so it doesn't get counted. That's the point—invisibility."

- *"Isn't this just a Paris problem?"* → "Paris is our proof of concept, but the methodology applies globally. Every country with aging population + young families + affordability pressures faces this. Japan, Germany, Italy—all have demographic crises that correlate with food affordability."

- *"What would fix this?"* → "Three things: (1) Minimum wages indexed to REAL food costs, not official statistics. (2) Family support systems that account for composition (more support for larger families, elderly care). (3) Policy makers using real data—like what Snipper Tool provides—instead of incomplete official baskets."
