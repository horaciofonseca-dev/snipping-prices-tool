## The Structural Deficit: SMIC Cannot Afford Living

### SMIC Family Reality (€1,750/month income)

| Expense | Cost | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| **Total Essential Costs** | **€1,824** | All 12 categories |
| **Monthly Income (SMIC)** | **€1,750** | Gross wages |
| **Monthly Deficit** | **-€74** | BEFORE emergencies or savings |
| **Annual Deficit** | **-€888** | Accumulates into debt |

### What This Means in Practice:

**SMIC families cannot survive on SMIC income alone.**

Even with:
- No emergencies (car repairs, medical events)
- No savings (impossible)
- No debt repayment (impossible)
- No discretionary spending (entertainment, vacations, hobbies)
- Minimal personal items (no shoes, coat replacement)

They are still **€74/month in deficit**.

### Government Transfers Become Structural Necessity:

**CAF (Allocation Familiale) + Housing Allowance:**
- Housing allowance: €200-300/month (varies by apartment size, location)
- Child benefits: €100-150/month per child
- **Total transfer:** €300-550/month

**With Government Transfers:**
- Income + transfers: €1,750 + €300-550 = €2,050-2,300/month
- Costs: €1,824/month
- **Result:** Break-even or small surplus (€50-500/month)

**This is why 22% of French households receive housing assistance.** It's not supplemental—it's structural necessity. Without it, the math doesn't work.

## Real-World Consequences: How SMIC Families Survive

### When Budget + Transfers = Break-Even, Where Do Emergencies Come From?

SMIC families face impossible choices:

**Option 1: Debt Accumulation**
- Credit cards for unexpected costs
- Informal borrowing from family
- Buy-now-pay-later services (increasingly used)
- Medical debt

**Option 2: Go Without**
- Skip doctor visits (don't fill prescriptions)
- Wear worn clothing (children outgrow before replacement)
- Delay dental care
- Skip haircuts and personal services
- Reduce food variety (revert to cheap calories)
- Defer home/car repairs

**Option 3: Family Support**
- Grandparents providing childcare (reduces official childcare costs)
- Relatives helping with emergencies
- Interest-free loans from family

**Option 4: Informal Work**
- Under-the-table jobs
- Selling items, providing services
- Gig work (Uber, delivery apps)

**Option 5: Reduce Other Expenses**
- No vacation (impossible anyway)
- No entertainment (free activities only)
- No hobbies or personal spending
- Children's activities limited to free/subsidized

## The Sandwich Generation: Structural Impossibility

A 45-year-old supporting aging parent (€334 food cost) + adult child (€304 food cost) + own household:

**Food costs alone:** €941/month  
**Housing (larger apartment):** €800/month  
**All other essentials:** €400/month  
**Total:** €2,141/month

**At Median income (€3,500):** Uses 61% of income  
**At SMIC (€1,750):** 122% of income = **IMPOSSIBLE**

Even supporting both elderly parent and adult child is difficult at median income. On SMIC? Mathematically unaffordable.

This explains why "sandwich generation" support is a silent crisis—policy doesn't see it, families absorb it quietly through debt and sacrifice.

## Policy Implications: Poverty is Structural, Not Behavioral

### Current Policy Assumption:
> "SMIC workers have income. If they struggle, it's because of bad choices or unexpected emergencies."

### Data-Based Reality:
> "SMIC workers structurally lack sufficient income. Government transfers are mathematically required. The deficit isn't caused by choices—it's built into the wage structure."

### Consequences of This Shift:

1. **Income Sufficiency is the Problem**
   - Not "help poor people make better choices"
   - But "wages must be sufficient for dignified living"

2. **Government Transfers Are Not Welfare**
   - They're structural necessity
   - SMIC workers are entitled to them as a right
   - Without them, system collapses

3. **Family Composition Requires Policy Support**
   - Families grow naturally (babies, aging parents)
   - These aren't choices
   - Policy must account for composition changes
   - Current support (CAF) doesn't scale with composition

4. **Health Inequality is Price-Enforced**
   - Poor cannot afford health
   - Healthcare costs (€75/month in budget) are compressed
   - Leads to preventable health crises
   - Creates downstream costs
